<objective>
Give the creator title, subtitle, and attribution text that lands in the exported PNG.

Purpose: this is the element that turns a coloured map into a post. `04-CONTEXT.md` D4-15 calls text
*"the most visible element on the map"*, and `04-04` widened the font specifically so the countries
this product is about can be named correctly.

The hard parts are not the rendering — `<text>` with `text-anchor`, `dominant-baseline`, `<tspan dy>`,
and `paint-order` were all **measured to rasterise** through this repo's export path. The hard parts
are: **measuring text without a DOM** (Vitest runs `node`, so bounds are character counts derived from
a recorded worst-case advance), **refusing rather than truncating** when text will not fit, and
**never building the `<text>` element from a string**, which is the one genuine untrusted-input risk in
the phase.

Output: a pure text module with derived bounds and a sanitiser, a text layer, a text-editing surface,
three allowlisted refusal messages, and a PNG gate proving ink lands where the title goes.
</objective>

  <context>
Your Eurostat reference sets the attribution in **small grey type**. `04-UI-SPEC.md § 4.2`
deliberately does **not** copy that, and the reason is arithmetic rather than taste:

- A second fixed ink such as `#4B5563` has relative luminance **0.0889**. For it to clear WCAG AA
  4.5:1 against a creator-chosen water surface of luminance `L`, you need **`L >= 0.575`** — i.e.
  **only near-white water would be legal.**
- The single ink `#111827` has luminance **0.0091**, giving **`L >= 0.216`** — every surface lighter
  than mid-grey is legal.

question unanswerable. Hierarchy is carried by **size and weight only**; attribution reads as
secondary because it is 20 units at weight 400 in a corner, not because it is grey.
